This is the path of a sale from the ad to the cart, as Meta's pixel sees it. We traced it on the live site on 25 September. The top line is what Meta can see; the bottom line is what the shopper meets. The ad click, the landing page and the product page all reach Meta, with one exception: two live ads land on a redirect that drops the click ID. Then the trail breaks. Add to cart is set up but never leaves the browser. Checkout moves to africanancestry.myshopify.com, where the click ID doesn't exist and Meta sees a new browser, so the checkout, and by extension the purchase, can't be tied back to the ad. Meanwhile the shopper meets an SMS popup right at Add to cart, and the address bar changes at checkout: five taps to checkout, one of them closing a popup. The result: in August, Meta ads got the same clicks as July and reported half the purchases. These are Meta's figures, not total sales; much of the drop is likely measurement.

The first possibility is the back end, and it comes first because everything else builds on it. The opportunity is the ad budget she already spends, working at full strength. Tracking: send Add to cart and purchases to Meta, and keep the click ID from the ad all the way to the sale, including through redirects. Checkout: keep it on her own address, checkout.africanancestry.com, with the privacy promise beside the button and no popups between the ad and the checkout. Site performance: product pages that load in seconds on a phone, and that search engines and AI assistants can read. When Meta can see every step of a sale, it learns who her buyers are and finds more people like them. The same budget works harder.

The second possibility is the storefront, desktop and mobile, in the language you're looking at. A faster path to cart: from the ad to checkout in three taps, with no banners or popups in between. Clear pricing: $299 and the results timing on the first screen. The privacy promise up front: the sample is destroyed and the DNA is never sold, right beside Add to cart, where today it sits on a sub-page. And simpler words: the shopper chooses their mom's line or their dad's line, in plain words, instead of starting from the test names. Some photos are stand-ins until we shoot real families.

The third possibility is the family experience, and this slide builds one screen per click. Results take six to eight weeks after the lab receives the swab, and we'd make that time part of the story. While you wait: the app tracks the kit to the lab, and each week brings a short read and a prompt to record an elder, so the family archive starts before the result arrives. Click. Invite the family: results arrive sealed. Customers can open them alone, or plan a family reveal and invite relatives to open it together, in person or on a call. Click. The reveal: a people and a place, told like a story. Stop here, and let her imagine what comes next.
